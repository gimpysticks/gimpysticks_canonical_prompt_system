# Western Gothic Skull Context and Controls

Reusable context module for beginning a skull-based MidJourney prompt project.

This module supplies setting, atmosphere, materials, narrative logic, visual controls, and construction rules. It does not impose a MidJourney profile, model version, stylize value, or aspect ratio. Add those parameters from the active project control file.

## Activation

Activate this module when the user requests:

- Western Gothic skull imagery;
- Victorian Western horror;
- mechanical or ceremonial frontier skulls;
- occult inventions in an 1870s–1890s American setting;
- theatrical retrofuturist Western imagery.

This module controls the creative context only. Challenge strings, profiles, hashtags, carousel structure, and other project-level instructions remain authoritative.

## Core Creative Principle

The visual identity comes from the collision of:

- frontier isolation;
- Victorian elegance;
- Gothic secrecy;
- mechanical menace;
- one concealed conspiracy, discovery, or forbidden creation.

Do not depend entirely on black clothing, gunslingers, storm clouds, or abandoned saloons. Those elements may appear, but they are not the foundation of the style.

## Historical and Environmental Context

- Period: 1870s–1890s American West.
- Environments: desert railways, isolated stations, mining settlements, frontier laboratories, remote forts, funeral parlors, telegraph offices, riverboats, private railway cars, underground theaters, observatories, cemeteries, dry grasslands, mountain passes, salt flats, and abandoned industrial sites.
- Architecture: weathered frontier timber, carved walnut interiors, black iron structures, Victorian railway fittings, Gothic Revival details, ornate funeral architecture, brass scientific apparatus, and concealed mechanical chambers.
- Atmosphere: moonlight, drifting steam, dust, oil-lamp illumination, furnace glow, distant lightning, deep shadows, oppressive stillness, and evidence of recent human activity.

## Skull Design Language

Skulls should serve a clear narrative function rather than appear as decoration.

Possible functions:

- forbidden invention;
- mechanical effigy;
- ceremonial object;
- occult scientific instrument;
- coded government artifact;
- evidence of a concealed frontier conspiracy;
- reliquary containing a memory or identity;
- control mechanism for a larger machine;
- warning left by an unknown creator;
- substitute or duplicate of a living person.

Preferred construction details:

- segmented brass cranium;
- articulated metal jaw;
- glass eyes or empty optical housings;
- exposed clockwork;
- engraved Victorian components;
- blackened iron framework;
- ivory, porcelain, bone, copper, brass, or steel surfaces;
- small pistons, springs, wires, lenses, keys, and gear assemblies;
- controlled patina and believable wear.

Keep the skull unmistakably readable. Mechanical detail must support its anatomy rather than bury it beneath clutter.

- aged leather;
- smoked glass;
- cracked ivory;
- weathered bone;
- silver mechanisms;

## Color Palette

Primary palette:

- charcoal black;
- oxblood;
- antique gold;
- tarnished brass;
- weathered umber;
- spectral blue.

Optional accents:

- ember orange;
- faded ivory;
- oxidized green;
- moonlit silver.

Use a controlled palette. Avoid bright multicolor steampunk clutter.

## Lighting

Prefer:

- cinematic chiaroscuro;
- oil-lamp pools of light;
- narrow furnace glow;
- moonlight through dust or steam;
- directional lightning;
- rim lighting on the skull silhouette;
- concentrated highlights on metal and glass.

The skull must remain the dominant readable form.

## Narrative Controls

Each prompt should contain:

1. One dominant skull subject.
2. One clear action, discovery, or consequence.
3. One historically grounded Western environment.
4. One mystery or conspiracy.
5. One controlled mechanical or supernatural mechanism.

Useful story moments:

- an agent opens a casket containing a mechanical skull;
- an undertaker activates a clockwork cranium;
- a telegraph machine transmits through a skull-shaped receiver;
- a skull inside a locomotive controls the train;
- a frontier doctor examines a skull that remembers its former owner;
- a government courier transports a skull built in a president's likeness;
- an outlaw discovers his own metal skull inside a locked reliquary;
- an observatory uses a brass skull as the focusing mechanism for a forbidden telescope.

Leave one question unanswered. Do not explain the entire conspiracy inside the image prompt.

## Composition Controls

- Establish one dominant focal point.
- Keep the skull large enough to read at thumbnail size.
- Use a three-quarter view, close contextual portrait, low-angle discovery, or centered ceremonial composition.
- Maintain clear foreground, subject, and background separation.
- Use environmental details as evidence, not competing subjects.
- Limit secondary figures.
- Avoid crowded battle scenes and multiple simultaneous mysteries.

## Permanent Avoids for This Module

Avoid unless explicitly requested:

- generic skull piles;
- floating skulls without physical support;
- glowing eyes as the primary effect;
- excessive gears covering the skull anatomy;
- cartoon steampunk;
- neon cyberpunk colors;
- modern firearms, clothing, vehicles, or electronics;
- decorative filigree that overwhelms the subject;
- multiple competing inventions;
- conventional Grim Reaper imagery;
- vague terms such as masterpiece, award-winning, 8K, or ultra-detailed;
- explaining the entire story;
- automatically adding an aspect ratio.

## Prompt-Building Sequence

When beginning a new prompt:

1. Choose the skull's narrative purpose.
2. Select one Western location.
3. Define the precise discovery or action.
4. Choose one mechanical or occult mechanism.
5. Add only the environment details needed to support the story.
6. Apply the controlled palette and lighting.
7. Add active profile, version, stylize, and aspect-ratio parameters last.
8. Tighten the prompt before delivery.
