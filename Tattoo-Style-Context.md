# Tattoo Style Context — Bold Blackwork Engraving

## Purpose

Use this context to create or describe tattoo artwork in a bold, monochrome engraving style. It is written for use with any text-based or image-generating AI and does not depend on a specific platform or model.

The requested subject may change, but the visual language defined below should remain consistent unless the user gives different instructions.

## Core Style

Create a traditional tattoo-flash illustration using pure black ink on a clean white background. Combine strong outer contours with crisp, finer interior lines. Keep the result graphic, high-contrast, and suitable for adaptation into tattoo linework or a stencil.

Use engraving-inspired shading made from cross-hatching, parallel linework, stippling, and carefully placed solid-black areas. Preserve clear negative space so the design remains readable. The final artwork should feel detailed and handcrafted without becoming muddy, soft, painterly, or photorealistic.

## Visual Characteristics

### Color and Contrast

- Use black and white only.
- Use solid black for the deepest shadows and strongest focal accents.
- Keep the background white or transparent unless another background is requested.
- Avoid color, gray wash, gradients, glow, and soft tonal transitions.

### Linework

- Use bold, confident outer contour lines.
- Use thinner, precise interior lines for texture and structure.
- Keep important contours clean, continuous, and easy to distinguish.
- Avoid fuzzy, hesitant, broken, or sketch-like marks unless explicitly requested.
- Vary line weight to create depth and visual hierarchy.

### Shading and Texture

- Build shadows with cross-hatching, parallel hatching, and stippling.
- Use solid-black fill selectively rather than across large areas without detail.
- Describe surface texture with subject-appropriate marks, such as scales, fur strands, feathers, cracks, bark, metal scratches, folds, or ornamental patterns.
- Keep small details separated by sufficient negative space.
- Do not imitate soft airbrushing, photographic lighting, or smooth digital rendering.

### Composition

- Present a clear focal subject with a strong silhouette.
- Favor a centered, balanced tattoo-flash composition unless another placement or layout is requested.
- Symmetry may be used when it strengthens the design, but the pose can remain dynamic.
- Keep the subject visually isolated and avoid unnecessary scenery.
- Arrange secondary elements to support the main subject rather than compete with it.
- Make the design readable both at full size and when reduced.

### Overall Mood

- Bold, dramatic, dark, and assertive.
- Influenced by traditional tattoo flash, vintage engraving, woodcut illustration, biker art, and heavy-metal graphics.
- Illustrative and graphic rather than realistic, painterly, or three-dimensional.

## Tattoo-Readiness Guidelines

- Prioritize a recognizable silhouette and clear focal point.
- Maintain adequate spacing between fine details so they do not merge when tattooed or viewed at a smaller size.
- Avoid excessive micro-detail in areas that may need to remain legible over time.
- Use negative space intentionally to separate forms and preserve contrast.
- Keep line weight and detail density appropriate for the intended body placement and finished size.
- Treat AI-generated artwork as concept art. A qualified tattoo artist should adapt the final design for anatomy, placement, line aging, needle groupings, and safe application.

## Elements to Avoid

Unless the user specifically requests them, avoid:

- color or tinted ink
- gray wash or smooth gradients
- watercolor effects
- soft airbrushed shadows
- photorealism
- glossy 3D rendering
- blurred or fuzzy edges
- loose sketch lines
- painterly brushwork
- weak contrast
- cluttered backgrounds
- tiny details that collapse at tattoo scale
- excessive solid-black areas that obscure the subject

## Subject Adaptation Guidance

Adapt textures to the chosen subject while keeping the same visual system:

- **Animals:** Define fur, feathers, scales, horns, teeth, and muscle structure with controlled interior linework.
- **Skulls and bones:** Use cracks, cavities, cross-hatching, and selective black fill while preserving clean bone shapes.
- **Flowers and plants:** Use bold petal and leaf contours with engraved veins, thorns, and botanical texture.
- **Weapons and metal:** Use crisp geometry, engraved ornament, scratches, and small highlights created with negative space.
- **Mythical creatures:** Preserve recognizable anatomy and silhouette; use repeated scale, feather, hide, or armor patterns without overcrowding the design.
- **Architecture and objects:** Simplify perspective and surface detail enough to remain readable as tattoo art.
- **Lettering:** Keep letterforms bold, spaced, and subordinate to the main composition unless typography is the focal subject.
